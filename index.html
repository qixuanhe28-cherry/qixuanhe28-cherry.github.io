About Me

Hi, I'm Qixuan ("chee-shwen"/Cherry)
Welcom to my website!

I am a Master’s student in Emerging Media Studies at Boston University. I study how emerging media technologies shape social perceptions, especially towards the "outgroups" based on gender and ethnic backgrounds. My broader research interests lie in the intersection of political communication, media psychology, and human-technology interactions.


First time presenting at the ICA, Cape Town, South Africa (2026)


Before BU, I was an undergraduate student at the School of Media and Communication, Shanghai Jiao Tong University. My career goal is to become a communication scholar.

Research

  • Research Toolkits
    Quantitative: Experiments and Surveys (Qualtrics, Pavlovia), Data Analysis (SPSS, Stata, R, Python), Biometrics (eye-tracking, electrodermal activity, heart-rate measures)
    Qualitative: Interviews and Focus Groups, Data Analysis (NVivo)
  • Current Research at BU
  • Research 1 - Implicit and Explicit Evaluations of Female Leaders in AI-Mediated Communication
    Principal Investigator (2025–present) · Boston University · STAR Lab (Dr. Joshua Baldwin)

    Using an experimental approach, my master’s thesis examined how media emphasis on warmth and competence, AI-versus-human source cues, and AI-related heuristics jointly shape impression formation of female political candidates.

    I also lead a project examining how people evaluate female engineering professionals on social media, comparing them with their male counterparts and contrasting content attributed to AI creators with content attributed to human authors.

    Implicit attitude (IAT) and attitude accessibility (Reaction time) are assessed in these studies.

  • Research 2 - Immigration discourses on U.S. Social Media
    Research Assistant (2026–present) · Boston University · CRSI Lab (Dr. Xiaoya Jiang)

    I lead a project examining how stereotypes and threat narratives about Chinese international students are produced and circulated in U.S. online discourse. This is achieved through human coding and computational text analysis such as topic modeling, sentiment analysis, and time series models.

  • Previous Projects at SJTU
  • Project 1 - Platformed Motherhood on Chinese Social Media
    Researcher (2023–2024) · Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Dr. Dong Zhou

    Drawing on interviews with women working in China’s short-video industry, I examined how mom influencers pursue entrepreneurial opportunities while navigating platform precarity and gendered expectations of care, femininity, and motherhood. This work first led me to consider how platforms structure the conditions under which social identities become visible, recognizable, and evaluated.

  • Project 2 - Emerging Technology & Entertainment Experiences
    Research Assistant (2022–2024) · Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Dr. Yitong Huang

    My earlier collaborative research examined how emerging media experiences (e.g. VR museums and esports gaming) contribute to entertainment gratifications and psychological well-being. I contributed to survey design, data collection, statistical analysis, and manuscript development. These projects provided my foundation in survey research, structural equation modeling, and media psychology while broadening my understanding of the positive outcomes of mediated experience.

Travel - Inspiration

I am from the Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture of Gannan (甘南藏族自治州), a culturally diverse region on the northeastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau in China. Growing up where Tibetan and Han traditions coexist in everyday life made me curious about how people from different backgrounds communicate, interpret one another, and build shared understanding.

Labrang Temple, Gannan, China (Summer 2026)

  • Exploring Europe

    During my exchange semester in the Netherlands (2024), I traveled across the country and visited France, Germany, Switzerland, Norway, Spain, Portugal, and Belgium. Meeting people with different cultural and social backgrounds strengthened my interest in group perception and communication. These experiences made the questions behind my research more personal: how do people understand those they perceive as different, how do stereotypes create distance and conflict, and how might communication foster recognition, empathy, and mutual understanding?

Research Goals

My long-term goal is to become a communication scholar who connects psychological mechanisms with broader social and communicative processes. I hope to explain how emerging technologies shape perceptions of individuals and groups, and when those technologies reinforce or disrupt social inequality.


My developing research agenda centers on:

  • Doctoral Study
    Pursuing doctoral training in communication, with an emphasis on social perception, media psychology, political communication, and AI-mediated communication.
  • Research Outcome
    Building a multilevel account of mediated social perception by connecting individual impression formation with the production and circulation of group stereotypes in digital environments.
  • Social Impact
    Producing research that helps explain and reduce stereotype-based judgment, supports communication across group boundaries, and informs more equitable technology and media practices.
